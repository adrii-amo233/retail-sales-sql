# Insights: Duka Smart Electronics

All figures use completed orders only and the revenue formula `quantity * unit_price * (1 - discount / 100)`. Data covers January 2025 to September 2026.

## 1. A fifth of buyers bring in nearly two-thirds of revenue
**Finding (Q45, Q22):** 28 of the 132 purchasing customers spend KSh 300,000 or more. Together they bring in KSh 15.4M of KSh 24.2M, which is 63.7% of revenue. The 73 Low Value customers (under KSh 100,000) add only 13.3%.
**Why it matters:** Losing a few top customers would hurt far more than losing many small ones.
**Recommendation:** Give the High Value group a named account contact, early access to new stock and delivery priority. Move Medium Value customers (31 of them) toward the 300,000 line with bundle offers.

## 2. Accessories make the money per shilling; phones and laptops make it per sale
**Finding (Q33):** Accessories have a 42.4% margin but only KSh 1.08M revenue (4.5% of the total). Mobile Phones, Computing and Electronics earn roughly 10% margins, and Home Appliances 11.4%. Mobile Phones still earn the most profit in total, KSh 728,761, compared with KSh 456,746 for Accessories.
**Why it matters:** Big-ticket items bring customers in but leave thin margins, and discounts can erase them.
**Recommendation:** Sell an accessory with every phone and laptop (case, charger, mouse) and cap discounts on phones and laptops so the margin stays above 10%.

## 3. The business is growing, but sales are very seasonal
**Finding (Q38 to Q40):** January to September 2026 revenue was KSh 12.8M against KSh 6.9M for the same nine months of 2025, up about 86%. December 2025 hit KSh 2.03M, then January 2026 dropped to KSh 322K, the lowest month of the 21 (Q46d). August 2026 is the best month so far at KSh 2.24M.
**Why it matters:** Cash flow and stock planning need to handle a big year-end peak followed by a January slump.
**Recommendation:** Build stock ahead of November and December, and run a planned January promotion (for example back-to-school laptops) to soften the dip.

## 4. Nairobi is the market; most other towns are small
**Finding (Q25):** Nairobi customers generated KSh 9.58M, which is 39.7% of revenue, and Nairobi has 48 of the 150 customers (Q17). Mombasa is second at KSh 3.16M and Nakuru third at KSh 2.39M.
**Why it matters:** The business depends on one city, but smaller towns are under-served.
**Recommendation:** Protect Nairobi service levels first, then test targeted campaigns in Mombasa and Nakuru, where there are already customers but far lower sales.

## 5. Over a third of buyers have gone quiet
**Finding (Q34):** 47 of the 132 purchasing customers have not completed an order in more than 90 days. They spent KSh 4.6M in total, about 19% of revenue. The longest gap is 625 days.
**Why it matters:** Winning back a past buyer is cheaper than finding a new one, and this group has already proven it will spend.
**Recommendation:** Send a win-back message (a small M-Pesa-friendly offer or a "new arrivals" note) to these 47, starting with the biggest past spenders.

## 6. VIPs spend the most, and 18 registered customers have never bought
**Finding (Q27, Q23):** Average completed spending is KSh 253,252 for VIP, KSh 197,959 for Corporate and KSh 163,575 for Retail. 12 customers have no orders at all (Q23), and 18 have no completed order.
**Why it matters:** The VIP label does track real value, and the inactive sign-ups are wasted acquisition effort.
**Recommendation:** Keep investing in VIP and Corporate relationships, and send the never-ordered customers a first-purchase offer.

## 7. One order in nine does not complete
**Finding (Q20):** 11.25% of orders are cancelled (90 of 800), and another 76 are still pending.
**Why it matters:** Every cancelled order is lost revenue and wasted handling.
**Recommendation:** Find out why orders are cancelled (stock-outs, payment failure, delivery delays) and follow up pending orders within a day or two.
